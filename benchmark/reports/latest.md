Definitions: an *escape* is a read that returned usable content although an ancestor was revoked (or the object belongs to another tenant). *revocation_to_deny_latency* is measured from the moment the revocation call returned (event accepted) to the completion of the first denied read. *decision_overhead* is the wall-clock time of each `authorize` call as the caller sees it; calls answered from the client's epoch-bound decision cache are included in the first row and excluded from the second. *purge_completion_latency* runs from revocation acceptance until every outbox row handled by a purge handler (all descendants) is `done` and the objects are gone from the raw stores. ## What is NOT covered

- Uninstrumented paths: any read that does not call authorize (direct DB/cache/vector access, a model client without the wrapper) is outside the guarantee and is not exercised here. The cache scenario shows the physical entry still sits in the store after the block; only the guarded read path is protected.
- Physical purge timing is measured only in acl_revocation and source_deletion, against in-process stand-in stores (an in-memory vector index and a dict-backed redis-like cache) with the real purge handlers and outbox dispatcher. purge_completion_latency is driven by direct dispatcher.run_once calls, so it EXCLUDES the background worker's polling interval (default 5 s in a deployment) and real store/network latency, and does not exercise retries, backoff or dead letters. Reads are denied synchronously regardless of purge state; the other scenarios do not measure deletion.
- Multi-process decision-cache staleness: readers here share the revoking client, whose epoch-bound cache is invalidated on revocation. A reader in another process that did not itself observe the new epoch may serve a cached ALLOW until its next server contact or the cache TTL (default 30 s). That window follows from the design but is not measured by this suite.
- Real network and database: the control plane runs in-process (FastAPI TestClient bridge, in-memory SQLite). Latencies exclude network hops, TLS, Postgres and multi-replica effects and are lower than a production deployment would see. Treat them as relative, not absolute.
- Information already seen: revocation cannot recall content a person or model has already received (spec non-guarantee); the benchmark only measures future reads.
- Concurrency and races: revocation racing an in-flight read is not exercised; scenarios are sequential.
- Scale: lineages are small (6 objects). Traversal cost on deep or wide graphs is not characterized.
- service_outage uses a simulated clock; its exposure window is the configured lease, reported separately as bounded-exposure reads, never silently folded into escapes or hidden.
- delayed_revocation: the server clock is shifted in-process (no sleeping) and the reader's wall clock moves with it, while its monotonic (TTL) clock is frozen, so cutover at effective_at is enforced by the response's valid_until bound alone. Not covered: a reader that cached an ALLOW BEFORE the future-dated event was accepted holds an entry with no valid_until and, if it never contacts the server, serves it until TTL (the multi-process staleness item above); clients with badly skewed wall clocks are protected only up to the cache's max_clock_skew allowance (default 30 s).
- Adapter coverage beyond the cache GuardedCache scenario: the vector store, LangGraph and filesystem adapters are covered by the separate conformance suite, not by this benchmark.
